# P1 Racer: design notes

Retro F1 game for the owner's 13-year-old daughter (her favourite team is Mercedes). Fun over realism, with real touches: tyre compounds, pit stops, weather, a real-inspired circuit. Plain JavaScript on an HTML5 canvas, no build step: open `index.html` in a browser.

## What it is now
- **Overhead, Micro Machines-style view.** Free steering: left/right (or A/D) turn the car, up/down (or W/S) are gas and brake. Fixed-orientation camera that follows the car with a short look-ahead and zooms out with speed (`TD_ZOOM` to `TD_ZOOM_FAR`; cars and scenery keep their pixel size). This was originally a behind-the-car OutRun-style game; that view was removed once the overhead one proved far more fun (the old code is not kept in the project; it is in git history only if committed before the removal).
- **First screen:** choose SINGLE RACE (pick a circuit, then the weekend) or CHAMPIONSHIP (left/right), and the **difficulty** (up/down), then Enter. A finished single race returns here. Difficulty (`DIFFICULTIES` in `game.js`, remembered while the game is open) scales the rivals' speed and steps down the pit help: EASY (rivals x0.92, pit limiter + lane assist, stop ratings 1.5x more generous), NORMAL (x1.0, limiter + lane assist), HARD (x1.08, limiter only), EXPERT (x1.15, no help: only a soft slowing to 80 km/h once in the lane, normal brakes, ratings 0.8x). It also shows on the tyre screen.
- **Championship** (`game.js`: `championship`, `beginRound`, `finishChampionshipRound`, `drawStandings`): one round per circuit in `TRACKS` order (six now), same six cars throughout, a fresh forecast and a normal weekend (qualify, then 5 laps) each round. Points 25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8 (`CHAMP_POINTS`). When the player takes the flag, `completeField()` runs the AI cars on to the flag silently so every finishing position is known (order = who crossed the line first, `finishSeq`). Enter on the finish panel shows the standings (race result with points, plus the table); Enter again goes to the next round, and after the last round (round 6) the top three get the trophy screen (see the end of this file), then a final screen names the champion. Escape on the standings screen quits to the first screen.
- **Circuits:** Silverstone, Monaco, Monza, Spa, Singapore (night) and Interlagos (`tracks/*.js`; Spa is a dense forest, `nature: 1.5`; see the Night racing and Interlagos sections). The circuit screen (left/right, Enter; Esc goes back to the first screen) shows a card with a map for each circuit: one row of tall cards for up to three circuits, a 2 x 2 grid of wide cards (map left, text right) for four, two rows of narrower cards beyond (`circuitLayout()`; six is 3 x 2); the controls panel is on the first screen only. Each track file holds its sections, `lineS` (where the start/finish line is), `pit` (pit lane distances and box positions; the entry is always at distance 0, the seam), `style` (`walls`: 'corners' or 'street'; `nature`, `ponds`, `buildings`, `aiPace`, `night`, `rain`), `mapTurns` for the overhead map, and section scenery flags (`stands`, `wing`, `garages`, `boards`, `harbour` side, `tunnel`). `loadTrack()` in `game.js` rebuilds everything for the chosen track.
- **Weekend:** qualifying then the race. Qualifying is one timed flying lap alone on the track (the AI cars are not drawn or simulated); the five AI times are shown as TO BEAT. The times set a six-slot, two-column painted grid (pole on the left), and the race tyre screen follows (pick again). AI qualifying time is calculated from their pace (`aiQualifyingTime`), not driven. Forecast is fixed once qualifying starts.
- **Start line:** 20 segments along the start/finish straight (`LINE_S`, `LINE_SEG`, set per circuit by `lineS`; at Silverstone the Hamilton Straight) so the grid (`GRID_GAP`, `gridSlot`) is on straight road; the gantry and chequers are drawn there. Lap counting uses distance driven vs `lineProgress`, not the track seam.
- **Race:** 5 laps (`LAPS`) on any circuit against 5 fictional AI teams (Apex, Vortex, Solaris, Nimbus, Verdant). 3-2-1 lights, lap/best times, live leaderboard with time gaps, finish screen, Enter restarts.
- **Tyres:** soft / hard / wet, chosen on the grid screen. Wear cuts speed and grip. Wear is faster at speed and in tight corners, and the same on the grass as on the road: the grass already slows the car, which is penalty enough (`TD_GRASS_WEAR` in `topdown.js` is 1; raise it to make cutting corners wear the tyres faster). Wet tyre is slow and wears fast on a dry track but is best in rain; softs slide in the wet.
- **Weather:** a random forecast per race (dry, light rain, heavy rain, or a change from one to the other at the start of lap 3 or 4). Shown on the tyre screen (it cannot be changed), warned a lap ahead, ramps in over ~12 s.
- **The start** (`game.js`: "The start" section, `launchAtGo`/`launchWatch`; `updateTopDown`): the gas works during the 3-2-1 (it revs the engine note) but the car is held. Gas down within 0.15 s before GO is a perfect launch; held down earlier gives wheelspin in proportion to how early (up to half the drive, fading over 1.4 s, with smoke, tyre marks and squeal) and "TOO EAGER - WHEELSPIN!". Gas down after GO times the reaction (under 0.25 s is a great start). Tuning: `LAUNCH_*` constants.
- **Pit stops** (`pit.js` draws, `game.js` has the rules): a drawn pit lane runs beside the Hamilton Straight (entry just before the line, fast lane on the track side, a row of six team boxes on the garage side, a low pit wall, garages behind, "PIT" painted at the entry). Drive onto the entry road (out past the edge of the track) and two aids switch on (`tdPitCommit` in `topdown.js`; race only): a **pit limiter** that takes the car down to 80 km/h within a fraction of a second (no penalty), and **lane assist**, which steers down the fast lane and out of the exit road, so only gas and brake are needed (any steering input takes over; look-ahead 800 units). Brake to a stop with the middle of the car on the teal line in your box (a fixed box towards the middle of the lane, `pit.playerBox` in each track file, 0-based: Silverstone 3rd, Monaco 4th, Monza 5th, Spa 2nd, Singapore 3rd, Interlagos 4th, so there is room to settle after the entry; the AI cars take the other boxes in order): how close you stop sets the stop time, 2.0 s (perfect) up to 4.5 s (500+ units off); PERFECT / GOOD / OK / LONG ratings. Cars in the pit lane do not collide with each other (`handleCollisions` skips them), so you can never be trapped behind a slow car or shoved into the pit wall. The crew come to you anywhere within `PIT_REACH` of the mark, so overshooting is slow but not a missed stop. 1/2/3 choose the tyres waiting for you, shown in a rack at the bottom. The rack starts on the tyres you are on (so you cannot forget to change from wets) and after every stop on what you just fitted. Pixel-art mechanics run out, change the wheels, and run back; a lollipop man holds red then green. The old four-wheel timing game is still in `game.js`: set `PIT_MINIGAME = true` to use it instead. The race keeps running while stopped.
- **Radio:** short messages across the top ("Box, box!", "Perfect stop! Crew need 2.1 s.", "Go, go, go!", "Tyres are fading", "It is raining! Wets would be quicker.", "Fastest lap so far"). `radio()` in `game.js`.
- **AI:** pick tyres from the forecast, wear them, and pit when worn out or wrong for the weather. They decide as they pass the pit entry, then use the pit lane like the player: fast lane at 80 km/h, brake to a stop on their own box (the five boxes other than the player's, in order, `aiBoxIndex`), wait 2.4 to 4.6 s, fit fresh tyres, and rejoin at the exit. See `aiPitDecision` and the `ai.pit` phases in `updateAI`.
- **Scenery** (`scenery-art.js` pixel art, placed by `scenery.js`): leafy, autumn and pine trees, bushes, rocks, flowers, ponds; tyre stacks on the tightest corners, armco on fast ones, hay bales on gentle bends; marshal posts; grandstands, the main building with a car park, garages, advert boards, chequered gantry. Solid objects (`TD_HIT`) cut your speed; ground-level ones are not solid. An invisible wall sits just outside the kerb.
- **Scenery guard** (`clearSceneryFromTrack` in `topdown.js`): at start-up any object whose footprint comes within the kerb (+ a margin) of ANY part of the track is removed, because the lap passes close to itself. The gantry is exempt. Logs a warning with what it removed.
- **Sound** (`sound.js`, Web Audio, no files): engine pitch climbs through five gears with speed, grass rumble off the road, tyre squeal when sliding, rain noise with the weather, wall thuds, countdown/GO beeps, lap tick, pit-wheel cues (perfect/good/slow), finish jingle. Starts on the first key press (browser rule); M mutes. If Web Audio is missing it warns once and stays silent.
- **Title music** (`sound.js`, the "Title music" section): a 16-bar, 25.6 s loop in A minor at 150 bpm, written note by note in `MUSIC_BARS` (lead as "name steps" lists, chords for the bass and arpeggio) and played live on chip-style channels: quarter-width square lead, thin square arpeggio, triangle bass, noise drums. No audio files. Plays on the first screen and the circuit screen, fades out when a session starts, M mutes it. Browsers need a key press first, so the first screen shows "Press any key to start the music" until then. Scheduled from `musicUpdate()` every frame (no timers); a bar that does not add up to 16 steps throws at load.
- **Effects** (`effects.js`): skid marks behind the player's rear wheels when sliding or braking hard (fade after `SKID_LIFE`, capped at `SKID_MAX`), spray in the wet (player and AI), smoke when sliding on a dry track, dust on the grass.
- **Minimap** (`minimap.js`): bottom right, the whole circuit drawn once into a small canvas sized to the track's shape, with the start/finish tick and a dot per car (team colours; yours teal with a white ring; AI dots hidden in qualifying).
- **Pixel art:** cars are drawn from text pixel grids in code (`sprites.js` rear view for the pit-stop close-up, `topdown.js` top view), recoloured per team and tyre compound. The player car is Mercedes-style silver and teal with no logos (fictional names for the AI).

## Files
- `index.html` loads the scripts in order. `game.js` race rules and HUD; `topdown.js` driving, map and drawing; `scenery.js` where roadside objects go; `scenery-art.js` their pixel art; `sprites.js` pixel-art car frames; `pit.js` the pit road, boxes and crew; `minimap.js` the minimap; `sound.js` synthesised sound; `effects.js` skid marks, spray and smoke; `tracks/*.js` one file per circuit; `night.js` the night layer; `title.js` the title-screen animation; `trophy.js` the trophy screen; `save.js` saved settings and records.
- `game.js` keeps race state (`raceState`: mode, track, select, countdown, racing, pit, finished, standings, trophy, champion), tyres, weather, pit stops, AI, collisions, HUD. Positions are tracked as `playerProgress` (distance along the track) and `playerX` (sideways offset, +-1 = road edge); `topdown.js` converts the car's 2D map position into those.

## Adding a circuit
1. Write `tracks/<name>.js` like `monaco.js`. Put the start/finish straight first (long enough for the grid and the pit lane), with the pit entry just after the last corner.
2. Find its `mapTurns` with a search (angle per section, total 360, a loop that closes without crossing, corners wider than the road). The search scripts used so far live only in the session scratchpad; the idea: random-restart annealing on the angles, cost = closing gap + near-crossings + tight corners + distance from sensible base angles.
3. Add it to `TRACKS` in `game.js` and a `<script>` tag in `index.html`. `computeMapPoints()` logs a warning at load if a map does not close or a corner is too tight.

## How the track works
- Track sections are `[name, enter, hold, leave, curve, scenery]` (curve > 0 turns right). The curve values drive AI cornering speed and tyre wear.
- The map comes from `mapTurns` in each track file: degrees turned per section, found with a small search so the loop closes (total 360) and does not cross itself. A new circuit needs its own `mapTurns` (`buildTopTrack` logs a warning if it does not close or a corner is too tight for the road).
- Heights (hills) are not used.

## Tuning knobs
- `AI_PACE_MIN/MAX`, `TD_AI_PACE` (AI speed in this view), `LAPS`, `COMPOUNDS` (speed, grip, life, wet behaviour), `FORECASTS`, `WEATHER_RATE`, pit timings (`PIT_RESULTS`, `PIT_MARKER_RATE`).
- Overhead sizes: `TD_ZOOM`, `TD_ZOOM_FAR`, `TD_HALF` (road half-width), `TD_CAR_W`/`TD_CAR_LEN` (car contact), `TD_HIT` (scenery reach), `TD_WALL`.

## Lessons
- Flicker/shake came from things pushing the car in opposite directions every frame (wall bounce, car contact steps, random bounce). Fix by easing toward the contact face with a force that fades to zero, never fixed-size pushes. Always give a stuck car a way out (gas gives a crawl against a wall).
- Node is not installed. Check without a browser using macOS `jsc` (`/System/Library/Frameworks/JavaScriptCore.framework/Versions/A/Helpers/jsc`) against a stub canvas.

## Working rules
Keep usage low: Sonnet, no subagents, `/clear` between big pieces, record decisions here.

## Night racing (Singapore)
- `tracks/singapore.js`: fifth circuit, anticlockwise street circuit, `style.night: true`. `night.js` draws a dusk-style dark layer over the finished scene (`NIGHT_DARKNESS`), cutting holes for the floodlit road and pit lane, lamp posts and each car's headlights, then glows, lit windows and head/tail lights on top. Any circuit can be made a night circuit by setting `night: true`.

## Interlagos (sixth circuit)
- `tracks/interlagos.js`: anticlockwise, short and twisty (Senna S, Descida do Lago, Ferradura, Laranja, Pinheirinho, Bico de Pato, Mergulho, Juncao). Player box is the 4th. Six circuits means a six-round championship, matching the six cars and six pit boxes (the championship runs all six).
- Map search lesson: the annealing moves keep the sum of the turn angles, so the starting angles must add up to +/-360 (negative for anticlockwise) or the loop can never close properly.

## Saving
- `save.js`: one JSON string in the browser's `localStorage` under `f1-save`: `{ version: 1, difficulty, bestLaps: { <circuit name>: seconds } }`. Local to the browser, no export (decided: not that kind of game). Loaded once at start (`saveData`); `writeSave()` runs when the difficulty changes or a lap beats the circuit's record (any session, any difficulty). Records show as BEST on the circuit cards.
- Failures never stop the game: unreadable, corrupt, wrongly shaped or out-of-range data, or storage being unavailable, each log a `console.warn` with the detail and fall back to defaults.

## Title screen animation
- `title.js`: a road strip along the top of the first screen with the six cars (the player's silver and teal one and the five teams, the same pixel art as the race) driving across it at different speeds in two lanes, plus a rippling chequered flag either side of the title. `drawTitleArt(t)` takes seconds; `titleTime` is set every frame in `frame()`. Tunables: `TITLE_CAR_SPEEDS`, `TITLE_CAR_GAP`, the flag size in `drawTitleFlag`.
- Sparks: when a car pulls level with the one ahead on the title road, a little burst of yellow/orange sparks flies off its rear (`updateTitleSparks`, `TITLE_SPARK_LIFE`). It happens a few times a minute. Decided against the cars reacting to key presses (would be annoying).

## Pause
- Escape during the countdown, qualifying, the race or a pit stop pauses (`paused` in `game.js`): the update loop is skipped, so nothing moves or ticks, and the engine and rain go quiet. A panel offers RESUME and QUIT TO MENU (up/down, Enter; Esc again resumes). Quit calls `resetRace()`, back to the first screen, which gives up the race or the championship (the panel says so). M still mutes while paused.

## Rain bias and the trophy
- `style.rain` on a circuit (Interlagos: 2) weights the weekend forecast (`rollForecast` in `game.js`): forecasts that start wet get that weight, a fully dry one gets 1/weight, the rest stay at 1. Without it every forecast is equally likely (Silverstone: 17% fully dry, 50% wet at the start; Interlagos: 6% and 71%). The forecast is rolled again once a circuit is picked, so the choice applies to single races as well as the championship.
- Finishing the championship in the top three goes to a trophy screen first (`raceState = 'trophy'`, `trophy.js`): a pixel-art cup in gold, silver or bronze, twinkling stars, confetti for the winner, and `soundFanfare(place)` in `sound.js` (full fanfare for gold, a short rising jingle for silver and bronze). Enter goes on to the final table. Below third, it goes straight to the table as before.
