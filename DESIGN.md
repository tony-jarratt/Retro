# Hexfire: design notes

Turn-based tactical mech combat on a hex map, single player against a computer opponent. Inspired by the feel of the Battletech tabletop game (heat, armour per body location, positioning and range), but an original game in an original setting. Plain JavaScript on an HTML5 canvas, no build step: open `index.html` in a browser. Retro look to match the other games on the menu.

The name is Hexfire. It has not been checked for existing marks yet (milestone 7). It lives in its own folder, `Games/Hexfire` (it was `Retro/Mech/` inside the arcade until 2026-10-25).

## Decisions so far
- **Design principles (the owner's).** (1) *You must be able to tell why you lost*: show the dice and the cause of every result. (2) *If you have to explain how a UX works, it is not a very good UX*: the screen should show what to do and what will happen (the banner, the yellow instruction line, the aiming callout, the range finder), not rely on help text or a manual. Easy, not simulationist; choices take effect at once.
- Overhead hex map for everything, no cut-scenes (see Visuals).
- **Turn based, sequential: A moves, B moves, A fires, B fires.** The first side is random in round 1 and the sides swap every round. A mech moves the moment you click its destination; a side's weapons are chosen one by one and then fire as one volley. Nothing is simultaneous between the sides, so a target you lock is a target you hit at the odds shown, and a mech destroyed by A's volley cannot fire back in B's. Nothing to confirm anywhere.
- Squads of 1 to 3 mechs per side. The player **builds** their squad in the mech bay by spending credits: one super mech or three light ones.
- **No machine persistence.** A squad is a short hex code you write down or send to a friend.
- **Simple damage: one armour pool per mech, plus criticals.** Every hit comes straight off the mech's pool; at zero it is destroyed. A hit has a 1 in 10 chance of a critical that knocks out one random working weapon for the rest of the fight. No hit locations, no lost legs or arms. The eight-location model (destroyed limbs, stripped limbs) is kept as an **advanced mode** (`DAMAGE_MODELS` in `data.js`; `?damage=locations` to try it), not deleted, because the project has no version control to recover it from.
- To-hit is d20 (it was d10 until 2026-10-07). Jump jets are a later version. Version 1 is a single encounter against a fixed enemy squad per difficulty; the campaign comes later.
- **UX principle:** easy, not simulationist. Preview everything before committing; automate what a tabletop player would do by hand.

## Where it could go next (the owner, 2026-10-25; all to be decided)
First: **play-test the story for balance** (`index.html?playtest` opens every mission). Then, in no fixed order:
- **Cut scenes in story mode**: short drawn scenes between missions (the dropship lifting off, the crawler leaving the canyon, the doors), beyond the text briefings and debriefs.
- **Player v player**: two people on one screen (hot seat), each building a squad in the bay. The turn structure already alternates the sides.
- **Ground troops, XCOM-style** (a significant change): infantry squads as well as mechs. Two shapes: (a) **on the same hex grid**, small units beside the mechs (cheap, hard to hit, good in cover and buildings, can capture or hold hexes, but a mech shot or a howitzer shell ruins them); or (b) **a zoom-in**: a hex such as a building, a hangar or the core's hall opens into a finer grid where the troops fight it out room by room, and the result feeds back to the mech battle. (a) fits the rules as they are; (b) is close to a second game. **The owner prefers (a), troops on the same grid, as the way to try it; parked until after the play-test.**
- Also raised: **pilot traits** (a small edge per named pilot), and balancing free play's enemy forces and heavies.

The game opens on the **mech bay** (M4 steps 1 and 2, written 2026-10-07, see Mech bay below); the default squad (`DEFAULT_SQUADS` in `main.js`) is a heavy Warden, a Jackal and a Skitter at normal. `?difficulty=easy|hard` previews the other enemy squads and `?code=<squad code>` plays any squad (see Test hooks). It is not part of the Retro arcade any more. The two throwaway fire-sequence mockups are still in `mockups/`.

**Where it stands (end of the 2026-10-06 sessions).** The owner is play-testing to "nail the combat" before M4. Since the first playable version these have been added, each described in its own section below: a simple **armour-pool damage model with criticals** (the hit-location model is kept as an advanced mode); **sticky heat** (slower cooling, bonuses for standing still and holding fire); **roles and terrain** (lights want water, heavies cover, mediums either) with the AI following them; **the dice shown on every shot**; **keyboard play** (arrows or WASD, ENTER); **smoke rockets**; **self-destruct**; **three weapon slots per mech with chassis capacity and weapon sizes**; the **laser array and howitzer** for heavy chassis; the **combat log** (what you did and what they did, kept until the next combat round) and a **one-card** right-hand panel (the mech in focus, or the one under the cursor). Tests: 201 checks in Chrome, 185 of them also under `jsc`; all pass.

**Next, in the owner's words and my judgement:** play-test and decide whether heavies now feel brutal and whether fights are explainable (the "why did I lose?" principle); then the mech bay (M4), which is when sinks, capacity, weapon sizes and the squad code become choices; the balance of one heavy against three lights needs the bay (the scripted squads buy no sinks) and a re-tune.

**How a round plays:** the banner at the top says whose step it is (`YOUR MOVE`, `ENEMY MOVE`, `YOUR FIRE`, `ENEMY FIRE`, in blue for you and red for the enemy) and a yellow line says exactly what to do. The combat log keeps the last combat round on screen (`ROUND 3: COMBAT`). *Moving:* each mech of the side is highlighted in turn (a pulsing ring, not a selection; for the enemy too, with its panel shown). For your mech the range it can walk to is **outlined in blue and the range it can run to in orange** (just the border, nothing filled in); hovering a hex gives the range finder (path, `movement 2 of 3`, `HEAT 0 -> 1`, a warning if the heat crosses a to-hit or speed threshold, and a ghost heat segment in the unit panel); **click a hex and the mech moves at once**; SPACE, ENTER or right-click = stay put. The enemy's mechs move the same way, one at a time, with a short beat before each. *Firing:* for your side, each weapon that has a target is asked about in turn (the focused weapon is marked `>` in the mech's card). The suggestion (the weapon's lock, else the nearest enemy) is shown with a dashed line and a callout (`NEED 7+ 40%`, every modifier, `LOCKED ON` or `LOCK LOST: reason`). Click an enemy to lock onto it, ENTER accepts the suggestion, SPACE or right-click skips the weapon, TAB or the arrows cycle targets, hovering an enemy aims at it, BACKSPACE undoes the last answer. Lines for the weapons already locked stay on the map. **When the last weapon is answered the whole side fires as one volley** (no summary, no confirm), played shot by shot with a camera push-in, tracers, beams, arcing missiles, explosions, floating damage numbers, screen shake and the **COMBAT LOG** panel (what both sides did in the last combat round); health bars drop as each shell lands. Killed mechs stay as smoking wrecks, and a volley that kills the last enemy ends the fight at once. *End of round:* heat sinks, shutdowns, ammo explosions, the win check, and the sides swap. The result screen offers ENTER = new battle and R = replay the same seed (every fight, including who goes first, is replayable from its seed, shown top right). Hold F to speed up. A failure of any kind (a plan the rules refuse, a bad map, the picture drifting from the rules) is shown on screen, in the console and in the page title, and stops the loop.

## Menus, maps and win conditions (2026-10-07)
**Flow.** A bare address opens the **title screen** (the hex-built HEXFIRE over the background picture, with two buttons, **FREE PLAY** and **STORY**; keys 1 / 2, left and right, ENTER). *Free play* goes to the **select screen** (`menu.js`, `drawSelect`): a card for each map (a small thumbnail with the starts marked) and each win condition, chosen immediately by clicking, then CONTINUE (or ENTER) to the mech bay and FIGHT; BACK returns to the title. The bay's top bar has a button showing the map and goal that opens the select screen again; on the result screen **M** does the same. *Story* plays the three-line fade intro (`intro.js`, below; the first time in a visit only) and then the **story screen** (see Story campaign below). `?code=` (a reload that keeps a squad) opens the bay directly; `?intro` plays the story intro; `?fight` skips every menu. The address also keeps `?map=` and `?goal=` (written by `syncUrl` with the code), so a reload keeps the whole setup.

**Maps** (`scenarios.js`; three starts a side; starts on clear ground; every start reaches every enemy start; each map names a `centre` for the flag and hill, `bases` and `exits`). Three themes, each in **two sizes**: **small** 19 x 12 (fits the screen, the quick one, the default for tests and the balance run) and **large** 31 x 20 (scrolls, see Scrollable maps; starts about 28 hexes apart). The themes: **The Basin** (small: the original, its flag and hill on the dry ground south of the lake; large: a big lake, a northern ridge, the flag and hill south of the lake), **Dead City** (terrain `B`, buildings: impassable, block line of sight, cover +2; blocks and streets, a plaza in the middle) and **The Range** (terrain `M`, peaks: impassable and block line of sight; hill ridges, passes and a stream). **The small maps are only for last mech standing** (`sizes` on each goal in `objectives.js`, `goalAllowsSize`): the flag, the hill and the exit need room. On the select screen SMALL is greyed (with the reason) while one of those goals is chosen, and choosing such a goal while SMALL is selected moves the selection to LARGE and says so; a `?goal=` in the address with no size gets the large map (a console warning), and an explicit small size with such a goal is a typed `size-goal` error; the rules, the AI and the tests can still run any goal on any map. The select screen shows the three themes and a **SMALL / LARGE** toggle (top right; it starts on LARGE, while a battle started with `?fight` and no size is small); the address keeps `?map=urban&size=large` (`?map=urban-large` also works); `scenarioFor(theme, size)` finds a map and the ids are `basin`, `basin-large` and so on. **Tried and reverted earlier the same day: 25 x 16 maps with smaller hexes** (the sprites shrank and the owner did not like it); scrolling replaced it. The large maps were generated with a script (a seeded generator plus a connectivity check; the checks in `checks-maps.js` re-verify every map).

**Scrollable maps (2026-10-07, `view.js`).** A map bigger than the map area is a picture of its own (`pixelSize`, with a 2 pixel margin so edge outlines are not cut) shown through a **viewport** (the map area, 790 x 568 px, about 19 x 12 hexes; `HEX_R` stays 26 so the sprites never shrink); a map that fits is centred as before and cannot scroll. Everything on screen goes through `hexToScreen` / `screenToHex` (`render.js`), which now include the scroll, so mouse, overlays, paths, ranges and effects follow it. **Controls:** drag the map with the mouse (a press that does not move is still a click, a drag never clicks), the wheel or trackpad, **Shift + arrows or WASD** (plain arrows still move the cursor), click or drag the **minimap**, **E** turns edge-of-screen scrolling on (off by default), **M** hides the minimap. The **minimap** (in the right-hand panel under the combat log, which shrinks to make room, so it never covers the map; large maps only: terrain, the goal's markers, every mech, a rectangle for the viewport; it was in the map's top right corner first, which covered too much) and **edge arrows** (a triangle on the edge for each off-screen mech and a loose flag) show what is out of view. **The camera** glides to the mech whose turn it is, frames where a move starts and ends, keeps the shooter and the target being aimed at in view, and before a volley snaps to frame every shooter and target; your own scrolling pauses the auto-follow until the next mech's turn; the view cannot be scrolled while a mech walks or a volley plays (effects hold screen positions). Test hooks: `?scroll=x,y` fixes the view, `?size=large`, and the key script tokens `drag:x1,y1,x2,y2`, `wheel:dx,dy`, `Shift+ArrowRight`; the `click:`/`hover:` tokens scroll their hex into view first. Small maps look the same as before (a headless screenshot comparison differed only by one colour level on the bottom edge and anti-aliasing). Measured on the large maps (average rounds, AI v AI): last mech standing about 10, flag about 7.7, hill about 7.3, exit about 8.1.

**Win conditions** (`objectives.js`; last mech standing is always in force, so a wiped-out side loses whatever the goal): **last mech standing** (no round limit); **capture the flag** (a flag on the centre hex; a move that passes through or ends on it picks it up; a carrier cannot run; a destroyed carrier drops it where it died; carry it into your own `bases` hexes to win; limit 20 rounds); **king of the hill** (the 7-hex zone round the centre; at the end of each round the side with more living mechs in it scores a point, level scores nothing; first to `HILL_TARGET` (4) wins; limit 15); **get to the exit** (each side has an `exits` group of hexes, in the far corner of the other half; **the whole living squad has to be inside its exit at once**, so the slowest mech sets the pace and a squad with a heavy loses the race to an all-light one; a destroyed mech need not get out; limit 15; this replaced "any one mech" because the fastest mech won the race in about 3 rounds). **Time limit** ("timed run"): at the limit the battle is decided on hill points (hill only), then total armour left, else a draw, and the result says so. The result screen always gives the **reason** (`state.reason`, from `reasonText`). Move-based wins are checked after every move (`finishIfOver` in main.js `performMove`, and the test runners), the hill and the limit in `endRound`; `objectiveSync` drops the flag when its carrier is destroyed. **AI** (`aiGoalFor` and `aiMoveToGoal` in `ai.js`, with roles): exit, every mech heads for its exit and runs; hill, every mech goes for the hill and a mech on it stays; flag, the carrier walks it home, the nearest mech fetches a loose flag, the nearest other mech escorts a friendly carrier, the slowest spare mech guards its own base (within 4 hexes, when there are enough mechs), and when the enemy has the flag everyone hunts the carrier and shoots it in preference to anything else. **The AI says what it is doing**: when a mech's job changes the combat log gets a line (`E2 HEADS FOR THE EXIT`, `E1 CARRIES THE FLAG HOME`, `E3 GUARDS THE BASE`). The balance report has a `GOALS` section (rounds per map and how each goal ends, AI v AI, every normal enemy force), a `FORCES` section and the `SHOTS` section. Measured on the three small maps (average rounds, AI v AI): last mech standing about 8, flag about 6, hill about 5.5, exit about 5.5 (it was 3 before the whole-squad rule). Backlog, not built: assassination (kill the marked commander), defend or survive N rounds, escort a VIP to an exit, destroy depots, salvage a wreck, waves, an exit that needs only N mechs (for story missions), a scrolling camera for maps bigger than 25 x 16.

**Enemy forces (2026-10-07).** Each difficulty has a pool of five enemy squads (`ENEMY_FORCES` in `data.js`: easy ESCORT, RAIDERS, GUNLINE, ROCKETEERS, BRAWLERS; normal HEAVY SCREEN, SIEGE LINE, STRIKE WING, ROCKET SCREEN, HAMMER WING; hard MONOLITH COLUMN, STEEL WALL, GHOST PACK, ARTILLERY PARK, STORM FRONT), each within 8% of the difficulty's enemy cost (87, 118, 144; checked by a test). The first of each is the squad the game always fought before; `ENEMY_SQUADS` still holds those. **A battle picks its force at random**, the **bay shows it** (an `ENEMY:` button with the name, cost and mechs; click it for a different one, never the same twice running), a new battle after a result picks a fresh one, R (replay) keeps it, and `?enemy=<name or number>` fixes it for tests; `pickEnemyForce(difficulty, seed)` is the seeded pick (its own random stream, so the dice of a seed do not change) used when nothing else chose. The result screen names the force. The `FORCES` report shows how each does against the suggested squads: they are not balanced against each other yet (for example STRIKE WING and ROCKET SCREEN beat the suggested squads 23 times in 24, SIEGE LINE loses most).

## Map features in free play (2026-10-26)
Asked for after the owner played the whole story: the repair bays "really add a dimension to the strategy", so free play gets them to try out, and what works goes into the story (or a new one) afterwards.
- **The switch:** a `FEATURES: ON / OFF` button on the select screen (off by default), kept in the address as `?features` and passed through the bay (its map button says `+ FEATURES`). The map cards' thumbnails show the features as dots (green bay, amber cache, blue tower). The battle log's opening lines list them.
- **What and where** (`MAP_FEATURES` in `scenarios.js`, placed by hand on all six maps): a repair bay and an ammo cache for each side about a quarter of the way in, the enemy's the mirror image of the player's (same row, mirrored column, so the walk is the same), and one **sensor tower** on the middle column, off the hill and the flag (on the basins an island in the lake). `scenarioFor(theme, size, true)` gives a copy whose rows have them; `featureHexes` refuses a feature on ground that cannot be entered or on a start or goal hex. Bays and caches are the story's own (`W`, `A`).
- **Sensor tower** (terrain `S`, `sensor: true`): while a side has a living mech standing on one, every shot that side fires is `SENSOR_HIT_MOD` (-1) easier (it shows in the shot breakdown as `sensor tower`; two towers do not add up). No stand-still rule. The header says `SENSOR TOWER: YOURS / THEIRS / NOBODY / BOTH SIDES`; the move preview says what standing there does. **AI** (`aiSensorGoal`): when the battle's goal gives a mech no job, a side holding no tower sends its one mech nearest a free tower within `AI_SENSOR_RANGE` (10), and a mech on a tower stays (`E1 HOLDS THE SENSOR TOWER`) unless it needs repairs. The AI already used bays and caches.
- Not tuned yet: to be play-tested. Next (agreed): fog of war as a second switch, with the tower also showing the enemy.

## Story campaign (2026-10-25, the Breakout slice)
The story, its pilots and the seven encounters are written up in `CAMPAIGN.md` (the owner's model: a fixed linear story, no mech bay, no carry-over, bespoke maps, an **access phrase** after each win unlocks the next; nothing is saved). **All seven encounters are built**, end to end (2026-10-25): BREAKOUT, THE DEPOT, THE COLUMN, DEAD GROUND, THE LAB, THE PARTNER and THE CORE. (Before they were built, a mission showed on the story screen as locked (a number only) or, once open, NOT BUILT YET.
- `campaign.js`: `CAMPAIGN` (the encounters: words, map, squad of named pilots, enemies, reinforcements, phrase), `encounterBattle`, `phraseUnlocks` (case, spaces and punctuation ignored; a wrong phrase is a `BuildError` shown on screen), and the story, briefing and debrief button layouts. render.js draws `drawStory`, `drawBriefing` (lines fade in, `briefingAlpha`), `drawDebrief` (the phrase in big letters).
- Flow: story screen (ENTER = latest open mission, P or the ACCESS PHRASE button = type a phrase) -> briefing (BEGIN / ENTER) -> battle -> result (win: ENTER = debrief; loss: ENTER = try again; ESC = story screen) -> debrief (CONTINUE) -> story screen with the next mission open. `game.storyUnlocked` counts the open missions for this visit. B and M (bay, map choice) do nothing in a story battle.
- **Escape** goal (`objectives.js`, `story: true` so it is not in `OBJECTIVE_IDS` and free play never offers it): the player's whole living squad has to stand on the exit (the map's landing pad hexes) at once, and at least `minOut` (2) of them have to be alive: once fewer are left the player has lost at once ("Only 1 of you left: it takes 2 to get out"; asked for after a play-test where one survivor of three won). Checked after moves and after every fire phase (`escapeCheck`), so a straggler destroyed with the rest already on the pad wins. The header says `SQUAD: 3 LEFT, 2 MUST GET OUT`; at the round limit the player has lost ("Time ran out after round 10"). The enemy has no goal and hunts.
- **Reinforcements** (`rules.js`): `state.reserves` = [{ round, mech }], made in advance and kept off the map; `arriveReserves` (called by endRound when a new round starts) puts each on its hex or the nearest free one (`nearestFreeHex`) and logs `E3 RANGER ARRIVES`. A side with reinforcements still to come is not beaten (`mechsLeft`).
- **Pilots**: a story mech has `pilot`; `callSign(mech)` is the pilot's name or the id. Cards, map labels, the log and the status line use the names (main.js `named` swaps P1, P2... in text); the enemy keeps its ids.
- **Story terrain** (`data.js`, art in `sprites.js`): `c` concrete (clear), `C` a stack of freight containers (like a city block: cannot be entered or seen through), `P` landing pad (clear; the escape goal's exit).
- **Breakout tuning** (`breakout` in CAMPAIGN): your three lights at armour 2 (VOSS Jackal Bolt+Flicker, WREN Skitter 2 Flickers, OKAFOR Jackal Rattler), against two unarmoured yard guards plus a Ranger and a Jackal arriving on round 4 at the south and north gates; you move first; 10 rounds. An AI that only runs for the pad gets out about 60% of the time (all three out about 38%); the first version (everyone at armour 1, the Ranger as well) was 30%. With the two-must-get-out rule (2026-10-26) it gets out 56% (169 of 300 seeds, was 181: the 17 lone-survivor wins mostly became losses; 2 remain where the last survivor also destroyed the last enemy, since last mech standing still wins). Play-tested by the owner with the rule: fun.
- **Raid** goal (THE DEPOT, story only): destroy every target (`targets`: the gun towers), then the **runner** (a named pilot, OKAFOR) has to stand in the hangar (`G` hexes). The runner destroyed = defeat; round limit 14 = defeat. Checked after moves and after every fire phase (`raidCheck` via `objectiveSync`), so the last tower falling with the runner already inside wins. Wiping out every enemy also wins (last mech standing is always in force). The hangar is dim until the towers are down, then glows and says who has to get in.
- **Gun towers** (`towers` on an encounter): enemy units with ids T1, T2..., `tower: true`, name GUN TOWER; built from an ordinary spec (a Bulwark chassis, so medium size to hit), `walkAllowance` 0, never asked to move (main.js skips them in the move step), drawn by `drawTowerAt` as a concrete emplacement whose turret turns to its target.
- **Depot tuning:** towers armour 0 (two Flickers and a Bolt by the hangar), garrison an unarmoured Jackal (Bolt) and Skitter; the AI playing your side wins about 56% (OKAFOR lost about a quarter of the time). A first version with a Bulwark in the garrison and armoured towers won 0%.
- Moon terrain: `d` moon dust (clear, little craters), `G` hangar floor (clear, hazard stripes).
- **Repair bays** (terrain `W`, any map; asked for after play-testing the depot, where a critical had cost a mech its weapon): a mech that **stood still** on a bay for the round (firing is allowed) gets, at the end of the round, up to `REPAIR_ARMOUR` (5) armour back, never above its start, **and** its first knocked-out weapon back online. Each bay has `REPAIR_CHARGES` (3), kept on the map as `map.charges` (hex key -> charges left, set up by makeMap); a mech with nothing to fix uses none. Armour is repaired only in the armour-pool game. Drawn as a green plate with a wrench and a pip per charge (greyed when used up); the move preview says what staying would fix ("REPAIR at round end: +5 armour, fix BOLT") or that it needs a round standing there; the log and a float say what was repaired. Either side can use them, and **the AI goes for them** (`aiRepairGoal`): a mech under half its armour or with a weapon knocked out heads for a bay with charges within 8 hexes and stays while it has something to fix (`E1 GOES TO REPAIR`, `E1 REPAIRS`), so a bay is something to reach first or keep under fire. The depot has two: a safe one behind the spires on your side and one by the hangar door under the Bolt tower. With them the AI playing your side wins the depot about 52% (it repairs about 2.4 times a fight; the detours cost it a little).
- **Escort** goal (THE COLUMN, story only): the **prison crawler** (`crawler` on the encounter: one of the player's units, id after the squad, pilot name CRAWLER, a Monolith hull with no guns, `crawler: true`) drives itself `CRAWLER_SPEED` (3) hexes a move along its **road** (`Q` hexes; `roadRoute` finds the route from `from` to `to`, a road that does not join up is a MapError), stopping short of the first hex anyone stands on (the log says `P4 BLOCKED BY E1`, shown as CRAWLER). It moves in your move step like a computer mech (main.js), is never asked to fire (fireplan.js skips it), never runs, and the AI never blows it up. Win: it reaches the end of the road; lose: it is destroyed, or the round limit (14). The header counts `CRAWLER: N HEXES TO GO`. The road is the contested thing: the enemy stalls it by standing on it, you can shield it with your own mechs (but they block it too). Drawn by `drawCrawlerAt` (tracks, barred windows, headlights).
- **Column tuning:** VOSS now has the Warden (Bolt, Rattler); escort two armoured Jackals (Bolt; Swarm); a Ranger and a Skitter come up the canyon on round 3; crawler armour 2 (66 points). The AI playing your side wins about 55%, nearly always losing by the crawler being destroyed (crawler armour 3 won 100%, armour 1 with the same enemy 37%).
- Canyon terrain: `s` shale (clear), `Q` road (clear, wheel ruts).
- **Defend** goal (DEAD GROUND, story only): hold out to the end of round `rounds` (8) with `protect` still standing (the **dropship**: one of the player's units, after the squad, `tower: true` and `ship: true`, so it never moves and is never asked to fire; pilot name DROPSHIP; drawn by `drawShipAt`, a lander with glowing engines, bigger than a hex). It destroyed = defeat. The header says `REFUELLING: ROUND n OF 8`. **Raiders** (`aiIsRaider`): the enemy's lights run for the dropship (to within 3 hexes, `GOES FOR THE DROPSHIP`) and shoot it in preference to anything in range; the heavier ones hunt as usual. So each round you choose between screening the ship and killing the raiders.
- **Dead Ground tuning:** HALE joins (Bulwark, Bolt and Swarm); the turnaround team starts with a Skitter and a Jackal (Scorcher) raiding and a Ranger fighting, then a raider from the south on round 2, a Bulwark and a Skitter on round 3, a Skitter on round 4 and a Jackal on round 5. Dropship armour 2. The AI playing your side wins about 72%: this is the story's breather. The result is very sensitive to when the raiders arrive (the southern raider a round later gives 92%) and to where your squad starts (bunched round the ship gave 59%, spread out 97% before the southern raider was added).
- **Heist** goal (THE LAB, story only): capture the flag one way. The crate (`crate` on the encounter, kept as `objective.flag`) is picked up by a move that passes through it, **only by the player**; the carrier cannot run, a destroyed carrier drops it where it fell; carrying it into the extraction hexes (the map's `P` hexes, `bases.player`) wins; the round limit (14) loses. The enemy has no goal and hunts, the carrier first (the flag code's carrier preference). Drawn as a banded case with a blinking light (`drawCrateIcon`), glowing on the ground, small beside its carrier; the log says CRATE instead of FLAG and the header `CRATE: WREN CARRIES IT`. An enemy spec may carry a `name` (the lab's PROTOTYPE, a Warden with the laser array).
- **Lab tuning:** HALE (Bulwark), OKAFOR (Ranger, Rattler and Bolt), WREN; squad armour 3. Two unarmoured guards and the unarmoured PROTOTYPE (Warden, ARRAY and Bolt); the alarm brings a Jackal and a Skitter through the east gate on round 5. The AI playing your side wins about 53% (many of its losses are timeouts: it is slow at fetching and carrying). Two security turrets were planned and dropped: with them it won 2%, with one 18%.
- **Ammo caches** (terrain `A`): the repair bay's twin for ammunition. ## Turn structure
**Decision history.** (1) The first design was sequential, and so is the current one. (2) Milestone 3 was built as *plan moves, resolve them, then aim weapons with the new positions known, then resolve the shots*; played, it was confusing (unclear phase, two rounds of questions per round, a "lock it in" step on everything). (3) The owner then preferred *give all your orders, then everything happens at once*, with a target that moves out of sight being lost (missiles unaffected); that was built (orders model, lockstep movement with collisions, lost shots, odds "if they stay put") and played. (4) The owner found it **not fun**: you lock a weapon onto an enemy, it moves out of range or sight, and the shot is lost. Sequential turns remove that, so the design went back to (1): **A moves, B moves, A fires, B fires**. All the work on the simultaneous version except the movement and orders model carried over (the map, speeds, AI, volley, effects).

**Revised 2026-10-07 (the owner felt the turns did not alternate, and they were right: the first side swapped every round, so a side acted twice in a row at each round boundary, and the side firing first could destroy mechs before they shot).** Each round now has three steps (`roundPhases(first)` in `rules.js`), and **the same side moves first every round**:
1. **First side moves,** one mech at a time in id order. A mech's move happens the moment it is chosen (`moveMech`): the hexes it enters are animated, heat from moving (walk 1, run 2) is added, and it is recorded as having walked, run or stood (which the to-hit odds use). A mech that is shut down or has no movement is passed over with a log line. A plan may not enter an occupied hex.
2. **Second side moves,** the same way, seeing where the first side went.
3. **COMBAT, both sides at once.** The enemy's shots are planned first (`aiPlanFire`, on the state as it is, so the plan cannot depend on yours), then you are asked weapon by weapon, then both lists are resolved in **one call** of `resolveFirePhase` and played as one volley. Every shot is judged on the state at the start of the step, so **a mech destroyed in the step still fires**, nobody has a first strike, and two mechs can destroy each other (a battle can end in a **draw**). **The damage is not drawn on the mechs while the shots land** (so you never see a wreck firing): the impacts show explosions, floating numbers, a brief flash and the log lines, and one beat after the last shot (`settleVolley` in `main.js`, 0.9 s) all the queued damage is applied at once: health bars, rocket heat, knocked-out weapons, wrecks and the DESTROYED and CRITICAL lines.
4. **End of round:** heat cook-off and shutdown checks, heat sinks, restarts, the goal's checks (the hill, the round limit) and the win check (`finishIfOver`, which the combat step also calls the moment a side has no mech left), then the round counter goes up. `aiPlanCombat` plans both sides for the tests and the balance runs.

**Who is first:** rolled once from the battle's seed and then **the same every round**, so the moves go A, B, A, B and the combat is shared (the owner chose this over swapping; it is the simplest to follow). The cost: the second side always moves with sight of where the first went. Simultaneous moves were considered and set aside (plan-then-resolve confused the owner before, and it needs collision rules). **The coin toss is shown:** the combat log at the start of a battle ends with `COIN TOSS: you move first.` (or `the enemy moves first`), and the result screen repeats who moved first next to the enemy force and the seed. **Measured (2026-10-07, 240 AI-v-AI fights per goal, the first mover split evenly):** the first mover wins 44% of last-mech-standing fights (the second mover sees where they went and nobody has a shooting edge), 55% of flag, 53% of hill and 60% of exit (it gets to the hex first). The owner's view: the second mover's information is a pro and a con against the first mover's ground advantage, and the order stays as it is. Cheap levers if a race ever feels unfair: let the second side move first in the race goals only, or let the player pick who moves first in the bay. Earlier design (kept for the history): four steps, first fires then second fires, first side swapped every round.

A side wins when the other has no living mech (shut-down and immobile mechs count as living). A draw is only possible if both sides lose their last mech to ammo explosions at the end of a round.

**Lock-on and follow.** Each weapon remembers its own last target across rounds, and that is its suggestion as long as the target is alive and valid (in range, in sight for lasers and cannon) when you come to fire. Otherwise the lock is lost and the suggestion falls back to the nearest valid enemy; the callout says why (`LOCK LOST: no line of sight`, and so on), so a changed suggestion is never silent. A skipped weapon keeps its lock; locking another target replaces it. First round: nothing locked, so the suggestion is the nearest valid enemy.


## Map and pace (from the first playtest)
The owner's first play: "mechs can move too far; the AI basically moved next to me in one go." Measured on the original 13 x 9 map: the closest pair started 8 hexes apart, every weapon reaches 6 to 10 hexes (so round 1 was already a shootout), mechs walked 3 to 7 hexes (running up to 10), and one enemy move left the closest pair 4 hexes apart.

Fixes, all three together:
- **A bigger map on the same screen:** 19 columns x 12 rows (THE BASIN), by drawing the hexes at radius 26 instead of 34 (`HEX_R` in `render.js`; the pixel art offsets scale by `HEX_SCALE`). The map is 754 x 563 px beside the 202 px unit panel. A lake in the middle and a ridge of hills in the north make a northern and a southern lane, with woods and rubble for cover and for breaking lines of sight.
- **Slower mechs** (table under Movement above).
- **The sides start on opposite edges, 16 or more hexes apart,** further than any weapon reaches (10), so nobody can fire on round 1 without someone having walked in.
- **The AI no longer runs.** Both sides running in from 16 hexes met in round 1. Running stays for the player (it costs heat and accuracy).

**No scrolling map, by decision.** A pan control, a minimap and click-to-hex under a moving camera would add the kind of interface the owner finds confusing, and 3 v 3 does not need more than about 19 columns. If a bigger map is ever wanted, `hexToScreen` / `screenToHex` are the one projection layer, so panning or zoom is a localized change.

**Relations to keep when tuning** (the checks in `checks.js` enforce them): the start separation is greater than the longest weapon reach; two mechs running flat out cannot meet in one move (`2 x longest run < separation`); the longest weapon reach is under 60% of the map width; every start can reach every enemy start.

**Result** (AI vs AI, 60 fights): with the player standing still, the closest pair goes 16 -> 12 -> 9 -> 7 -> 4 over four enemy moves (was 8 -> 4 in one); the first shot is on round 2 every time (was round 1); a fight lasts about 11 rounds.


## Rules (simplified, original)
Every number lives in `js/data.js`, so tuning never means touching rules code. Numbers there are starting points.

**Movement.** Stand, walk or run. A mech has a walking allowance in movement points (its chassis speed); running is 1.5x, rounded down. Entering a hex costs its terrain cost (clear 1, woods 2, hills 2, rubble 2, water 2). A mech cannot enter a hex holding another living mech. Walking adds 1 heat, running 2. Heat of 14 or more costs a movement point, 20 or more two. One lost leg halves speed and forbids running; two lost legs leave the mech immobile. **Speeds were halved after the first playtest** (see Map and pace): Skitter 5, Jackal 4, Ranger 3, Bulwark 3, Warden 2, Monolith 2 (running 7, 6, 4, 4, 3, 3), so that a round's move is a fraction of the way across the map.

**Heat.** A meter from 0 to 30. Weapons and moving add heat. At the end of each round the mech's heat sinks (chassis sinks plus any extra bought) remove heat, plus 2 for standing still and 2 for standing in water. From 8 a to-hit penalty (+1, +2 from 14, +3 from 20). From 20, ammo may cook off at the end of a round (a roll, then damage straight to the structure of the location holding that ammo). At 30 at the end of a round the mech shuts down: it cannot move or fire and is easier to hit (-2 to the target number) until it cools below 15 at the end of a later round.

**To-hit (a d20 since 2026-10-07, reworked the same day).** Roll a d20 and need to equal or beat a target number; the chance is `(21 - number) x 5%`, the number clamped to 3 to 19. The number is the pilot's **base to-hit** (called gunnery until 2026-10-07, which read as higher-is-better; here lower is better, like the `5+` you need to roll) (**5** for you; the enemy's is 7, 5 or 3 by difficulty) plus: **range** (+1 for every 2 hexes after the first: 1-2 hexes +0, 3-4 +1, 5-6 +2, 7-8 +3, 9-10 +4, the same for every weapon; each weapon's own reach only sets how far it can fire), **target size** (light +2, medium 0, heavy -2, assault -4), attacker moved (walk +2, run +4), target moved (walk +2, run +4; a cannon doubles it, the howitzer triples it), attacker heat (+2, +4, +6), cover (target in woods +2), -4 if the target is shut down, +2 for a missile fired without line of sight. **A natural 1 is a fumble:** it misses and the weapon jams (cannot fire in the shooter's next fire step; `JAM_ROUNDS` 2 counts round ends; shown as `JAMMED` on the card and `FUMBLE` in the log). **A natural 20 is a critical hit:** it hits and knocks out one random working weapon on the target for good (pool damage model only; replaces the old separate 1-in-10 critical roll, so it is 1 shot in 20). Why the numbers: the owner found "you miss a lot" (a mover shooting a mover at medium range needed 15, 30%); the AI-v-AI shot report in the balance run (`shotReport`) showed 42% hits on average, and lowering every gunnery by 4 plus the size modifiers gives about 48% on easy, 53% normal, 58% hard (the average player shot is about 52%). Fights got shorter (8 to 9 rounds). The d10 to d20 conversion before this was exact (modifiers doubled, gunnery 2n - 1). A weapon cannot fire at a target inside its minimum range, beyond its long range, or without line of sight.

**Line of sight.** Traced hex to hex. Any woods hex strictly between blocks it. A hills hex strictly between blocks it unless one end stands on a hill.

**Damage (the simple game, the default).** Each mech has one pool of armour points: `hp = round((its armour + structure over all eight locations) x POOL_SCALE)`, with `POOL_SCALE` 0.4 (a hit-location mech died when its head or centre torso went, which takes far less than all of its points; 0.6 gave fights of 17 rounds at the median, 0.4 gives 12). The armour level chosen in the build, and the chassis, set the pool. A hit takes its damage straight off the pool (clamped at what is left); at zero the mech is destroyed. No location is rolled, so a shot rolls to hit (and a cluster for missiles) and nothing more. **Criticals:** after the packets of a shot that hit, one extra d10 is rolled (one roll per shot, however many missiles landed); `CRIT_ROLL` (10) or more knocks out one random weapon of the target that still works, for the rest of the fight (`off` on the weapon; the panel shows `OFF`). A mech that has just been destroyed takes no critical, and a mech with no working weapon left takes none. An ammo explosion damages the mech's pool directly. A mech that can only move (every weapon knocked out) is still in the fight. **Stalemate:** if no mech on either side can ever fire again (every weapon knocked out, destroyed or out of ammo; a shut-down mech with ammo still counts), the fight is a draw.

**Hit locations (the advanced mode, `model: 'locations'`).** Each mech has the eight locations below, and none of the pool rules above apply.

**Stripped limbs (advanced mode only).** An arm or leg goes out of action as soon as its **armour** is shot away (a hit takes it to zero, or hits it when it had none; `stripped` on the mech), long before its structure is gone: a stripped arm cannot use its weapons (the panel shows `OFF`); one stripped leg halves the mech's speed and stops it running; **both legs stripped stop it moving**. Structure behind the armour still absorbs hits, and when it is gone the limb is destroyed and the excess carries inward, as before. Torsos and the head are not put out of action by losing their armour, rear hits take the rear armour and strip nothing, and an ammo explosion goes through the armour without stripping it. A limb built with no armour works until it is first hit.

**Facing.** A mech faces the way it last moved, and turns for free to face the target of its first weapon that fired. A hit is "from behind" when the attacker lies in the 120 degree wedge behind the target (the wedge edges count as side hits).

**Weapons** (seven, original names; stats in `data.js`):
- Lasers (Flicker, Bolt, Scorcher): no ammo, high heat, reliable.
- Autocannons (Rattler, Hammer): ammo, low heat, steady damage.
- Missile pods (Sting 4, Swarm 6): ammo, minimum range, one to-hit roll, then a cluster roll decides how many missiles land, and each lands on its own random location.
Each weapon has damage, heat, ammo, minimum range and three range bands.

**Terrain.** Clear, woods (cover, slow, blocks line of sight), hills (slow, height), water (slow, and **cools a mech a lot**: 5 extra heat removed at the end of a round for a mech standing in it, about double its normal cooling; `cool` in `TERRAIN`), rubble (slow).

## Mech bay (budget build)
- **One resource: credits.** The player's budget is fixed per difficulty (140 easy, 120 normal, 100 hard, to tune). Parts are unlimited; only the budget limits you.
- A squad is 1 to 3 mechs. Each mech is a **chassis** (base cost; sets speed, heat sinks, structure, armour scale and a weapon `capacity` of 3, 5, 6 or 7 slots), an **armour level** from 0 to 7 (one number per mech; it is spread across locations by a fixed pattern and costs more per level on heavier chassis), **extra heat sinks** (0 to 7, 3 credits each) and **up to three weapons** whose sizes (1 to 3 slots each) fit the chassis's capacity.
- One resource only, no separate tonnage. Capacity and the three-attack cap are the shape limit, credits the single trade-off. The screen shows a running total, a heat warning (warn, never forbid) and a one-line summary.
- **Balance goal:** one big mech must be competitive with three small ones. Simultaneous turns do not even out squad sizes (a lone big mech faces three volleys at once; three small mechs get three move-and-fire actions), so credit costs, armour scaling and the balance runs in the tests handle it. Knobs if one side dominates: armour cost, chassis capacity, weapon sizes and costs, the credit budget, the cooling rate and heat limits.

**Chassis** (original; capacity 3, 3, 5, 5, 6, 7 weapon slots): Skitter and Jackal (light), Ranger and Bulwark (medium), Warden (heavy), Monolith (assault). See Weapon slots, capacity and the heavy weapons for the table.

### Squad code (no persistence, by decision)
No `localStorage`, no files. The squad is a **hex code** shown in groups of 4, for example `3135-22A4-0481-0012-0400-10`, that you can write down or send to a friend. The mech bay has a Code box to show it and to paste one in. A friend who enters the same code plays the same squad against the same enemy.

**The algorithm (`js/code.js`: `encodeSquad` and `decodeSquad`).** It is plain bit packing, with a checksum, and no lookup tables, so it cannot drift out of step with the data except through the ids themselves.

| Field | Bits | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| version | 4 | `CODE_VERSION`, now 3 (2 had a weapon slot per chassis slot); a code of another version is refused with its version named |
| difficulty | 4 | 0 easy, 1 normal, 2 hard (`DIFFICULTIES[...].code`) |
| mech count | 4 | 1 to `SQUAD_MAX` (3) |
| per mech: chassis | 4 | chassis id, 1 to 6 (ids 1 to 15 would fit) |
| per mech: armour level | 3 | 0 to 7 |
| per mech: extra heat sinks | 3 | 0 to 7 |
| per mech: weapon slots | 3 x 4 | weapon id per slot, 0 = empty (ids 1 to 15 would fit) |

*Encode:* validate the squad first (`validateSquad`: so an illegal or over-budget squad has no code); push each field, most significant bit first, onto a list of bits; pad with zeros to a whole number of hex digits (4 bits each); turn each group of 4 bits into one hex digit; append the checksum as two more digits; show the whole string in groups of four joined by dashes. A 3-mech squad is 12 + 3 x 22 = 78 bits, padded to 80 = 20 digits, plus 2 = **22 digits**. A 1-mech squad is 34 bits, so 9 digits plus 2.

*Checksum:* take the data digits (everything but the last two), multiply the digit at position `i` (from 0) by the odd weight `2i + 1` (1, 3, 5, 7...), add them up, take the result mod 256, and write that as two hex digits. Any single wrong digit changes the sum by (odd weight) x (1 to 15), which is never a multiple of 256, so one mistyped digit is always caught; two neighbouring digits swapped is usually caught (their weights differ) but that is not guaranteed.

*Worked example, `3135-2090-0882-0012-0440-A8`:* the 20 data digits are `3135 2090 0882 0012 0440`, which is the 80 bits `0011 0001 0011 | 0101 001 000 0010 0100 0000 | 0010 001 000 0010 0000 0000 | 0001 001 000 0001 0001 0000 | 00`. Read in order: version 3, difficulty 1 (normal), 3 mechs; mech 1 chassis 5 (Warden), armour 1, 0 extra sinks, weapons 2 (Bolt), 4 (Rattler), 0; mech 2 chassis 2 (Jackal), armour 1, weapons 2, 0, 0; mech 3 chassis 1 (Skitter), armour 1, weapons 1 (Flicker), 1, 0; then 2 bits of zero padding. The checksum sum is 168 = `A8`.

*Decode:* strip spaces, dashes and case (people retype these); check every character is 0 to 9 or A to F and that there are at least 5 digits; recompute the checksum over all but the last two digits and compare; turn the digits back into bits and read the fields in order, checking each id exists (unknown chassis or weapon, a mech count outside 1 to 3, an unknown difficulty or version); require the bits left over to be fewer than 4 (anything more is extra digits) and all zero (padding); and finally validate the squad against the rules and the difficulty's budget. Every failure is a `BuildCodeError` with a distinct `kind` (`length`, `chars`, `checksum`, `version`, `difficulty`, `count`, `chassis`, `part`, `padding`, or the build's own kind such as `budget`), and the screen shows the message. It never falls back to a default squad silently.

*Why it is this long:* the weapon slots take 12 of each mech's 22 bits because every slot gets 4 bits though there are only 10 weapons. Base 32 digits would make a 3-mech code about 16 characters, and indexing each mech's loadout into a table of legal loadouts would be shorter still, but that table would have to change whenever a weapon is added, so it was not done. A longer code is fine because it is copied and pasted far more often than typed. If the id space or the fields change, bump `CODE_VERSION` so old codes are refused loudly.

## Screens and flow
1. **Title:** title art, then Custom Battle.
2. **Mech bay:** choose difficulty, build the squad (chassis, armour, sinks, weapons) within the budget, with the running cost, heat warning and the Code box.
3. **Battle:** the hex map, a banner and instruction line, one mech card (armour and heat bars with a ghost of the planned heat, the weapon list, movement) for the mech in focus, and the combat log. ## AI
Plain, explicit and easy to read, not a search. It moves one mech at a time from the state as it is at that moment (`aiMoveFor`), then fires its whole side (`aiPlanFire`):
1. **Move, walking only:** a mech that has nothing it can shoot from where it stands **heads for the nearest hex it could shoot the nearest enemy from** (a hex in weapon reach with a line of sight), working out the route across the whole map and walking as far along it as one move allows. A mech that can already shoot something fine-tunes within one move: the reachable hex closest to a firing range of 4 hexes, with a view of the target (a missing view counts as 3 hexes further off) and cover if it can. It stands still to cool at heat 14. It never runs.
2. **Fire:** each weapon at its suggestion (lock, else nearest) from the real positions; hold a weapon rather than take the mech past heat 26.
Difficulty changes the enemy's base to-hit (and the enemy squad). Each decision gives a one-line reason (`console.debug`) so odd behaviour can be diagnosed. The test fight runner uses this same AI for both sides, which is what the balance runs measure.

## Lessons
- **Plans never rely on another mech getting out of the way.** A move plan may not enter a hex that is occupied when the plan is made, even by a mech that means to leave. Lockstep movement still lets a mech enter a hex another leaves in the same step, but only if that hex was empty at the start.
- **Line of sight is symmetrical** (the line is always traced from the same end) **and ignores off-map hexes.** A line along the map edge can graze a hex just outside it; that hex has no terrain, so it cannot block.
- **To-hit odds shown on screen are the odds used.** `targetStatus` is the one place the number is worked out; `resolveFirePhase` takes it from there (a check covers this).
- **Order independence is tested.** Shots are rolled in a fixed order (mech id, then weapon slot), blocks in a move step are decided from one snapshot, and a check shuffles the mech and plan lists and compares the results.
- A weapon name that is also a Battletech term is a trademark risk even when it is an ordinary word. "Lance" (a four-mech unit there) became "Bolt".

- **A flat-top hex is 2 radii wide but only about 1.73 tall.** Fit checks use the half-height (`HEX_R x sqrt(3) / 2`), not the radius.
- **Size the map, the speeds and the weapon reach together.** Each was reasonable alone (13 hexes wide, 3 to 7 hexes a move, weapons reaching 6 to 10); together a mech could cross the map and fire in one round. The relations are now tests (see Map and pace).
- **Local scoring stalls; give an AI that has nothing to shoot a goal.** Two stalls showed up only when whole fights were run to the end (round 156, no result): first a slow mech one hex outside its reach would not step onto a hex that lost its view, then a mech at exactly the right range stood behind a wood. Scoring only the hexes one move away cannot find a way out of "right distance, no view"; heading for the nearest firing position across the whole map can. A test now starts a mech from several places against a player who stands still and requires it to fire within 14 rounds.
- **Run whole fights to the end in the real UI, not just unit tests.** The round-156 stall and the wrongly invalid shot both showed up in a full headless fight.
- **Judge every shot on the state before the volley.** An early version evaluated each shot as damage was being applied, so a target killed by one shot made the next shot "invalid" (spotted in the on-screen log of a full fight). `resolveFirePhase` computes all the statuses first (`validateFirePlan`); a test covers it.
- **Ask why a confusing screen is confusing before rewriting it.** The owner's complaints were about the round structure (two question rounds, lock-in steps, simultaneity they could not see), not just the layout. The fix was fewer decisions in one pass with an obvious banner, not a different engine; the sequential rewrite that was started and dropped cost an afternoon.
- **Check the thing the owner distrusts, not just the thing you built.** The owner suspected the paper doll was why combat "was not working". Replacing eight locations by one pool changed the balance picture far more than any tuning had (squad shapes went from 66/86/88% to 46/56/50%) and removed a source of snowballing and one-hit luck. A damage model is a tuning knob for fun, not just for realism.
- **No version control means flags, not deletes.** The location model stays behind `model: 'locations'` rather than being removed, and the rules tests default to it so it stays covered; the replay tests run both models.
- **A mechanic can pass every test and still be not fun.** The simultaneous orders build was correct, tested and playable, and the owner still found it not fun because a locked target could walk away. Play-test the *feel* before building a lot on a rule; a sequential round is also far simpler to explain and to build (no collisions, no hypothetical positions, no lost shots).
- **Do not let "unanswered" and "skipped" share a JSON-unsafe value.** The undo snapshot turned `undefined` (not answered yet) into `null` (skipped); the fire planner now uses `false` for unanswered, and a test checks that Back restores it.
- **Replay the damage in the order the screen plays it.** Each hit's `steps` assume the earlier hits on that target (in the rules' order: mech id, then weapon slot) have been applied. The volley staggers shots for readability, so two hits on one location could land in a different order and push armour below zero. `buildVolley` holds a later hit just behind an earlier one on the same target (`VOLLEY_ORDER_GAP`), `updateVolley` plays impacts by time then rules order, and `applyDamageEvent` throws `volley-drift` rather than show wrong numbers. The first version of the test replayed in rules order and could not see this; the test that replays the way the screen does can (checked by removing the fix).
- **The picture is a copy.** Before each phase resolves, `main.js` takes `cloneState`; the animation draws the copy and updates it as things happen, then drops it. That also fixed M2's heat-shown-too-early quirk.
- **The AI's mechs plan one after another and keep off each other's chosen paths.** Before this, two enemy mechs regularly blocked each other (seen in the log).
- **Headless Chrome and animation:** `--dump-dom` does not wait for the virtual-time budget (only screenshots do), and a screenshot sees about 30 frames whatever the budget. Hence `?dt` and `?turbo`.

## Balance (first numbers, M1)
The balance run puts one assault mech against three jackals, each built to the same budget, and fights 50 times with the sides swapped, using the scripted planner. Latest result, with the real AI playing both sides under the sequential rules (the single mech's share of decided fights): budget 140, 26%; 120, 69%; 100, 94%; a few timeouts at the lower budgets (after the slower speeds, the bigger map and the new AI). Earlier runs gave 28/88/92 (a scripted planner), then 6/52/91 (the AI with move-then-fire turns), so the numbers move a lot with how the sides play and with the turn rules; that is a reason not to tune hard against them. Read this as: the cost model is in the right area, but which archetype wins flips with the budget. At low budgets the trio pays for three chassis (3 x 18 credits) and has little left for weapons; at higher budgets it can afford good armour and three sets of weapons. The test builder also picks weapons by a fixed priority, so its builds jump between budgets and the curve is rough. Armour cost per level now follows the points a level buys (light 2, medium 3, heavy 4, assault 5), which narrowed the gap. Do not tune hard against these numbers; play the real game first. Knobs if one archetype dominates: chassis base cost (Monolith 60, Jackal 18), armour cost, slots on heavy chassis, sink cost, the budgets. With a human who stays put and accepts every suggestion against the AI (80 fights): the enemy wins about three in four, the first shot is always on round 2, and a fight takes 16 rounds at the median (longest 32). Sixteen rounds may be long; see the open questions.

## Roles, weapon identities and legibility (added after play-testing the armour-pool build)

**Principle: you must be able to tell why you lost.** A play-test was lost with many misses and no clue what went wrong. Measured over 80 AI-vs-AI fights: average hit chance 42%, 84% of shots at medium range, so nothing in a fight was decided by a visible choice. The fix is roles and visible causes, not more dice.

**The vision (owner's narrative).** Lights rush to water so their long-range, low-damage lasers can keep firing (water cools). Heavies hold the woods, which hide them from lasers (no line of sight), and lob rockets that need none. Rockets *disable* by heating the target. Lights get cooked, and the heavies finish it as a slow cannon duel. **Mediums are hybrids:** they can play either role, so they are the flexible pick.

| Class | Home terrain | Weapons it wants | Counter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | water (cooling) | lasers (longest direct reach) | rockets heat them; cannons are slow, but lights that stand still are easy prey |
| Medium | either water or cover | a mix | none; flexibility |
| Heavy / assault | woods and cover | cannons and rockets | lasers pin them if they leave cover |

**Rules added.** Lasers reach 9 at long range (cannons 6); cannons have scarce ammo (Rattler 14, Hammer 8) and `moverFactor` 2 (a target that walked is +2 to hit, ran +4); rockets add `heatOnHit` (1 per missile that lands, capped at 30) to the target, in both damage models, and the volley replay mirrors it.

**AI roles** (`terrainAffinity` in `ai.js`): a bonus for standing on water (light 2, medium 1.25), cover (heavy 2, medium 1.25, light 0.5), used both when fine-tuning a firing hex and when heading for one. Rockets default to the hottest target.

**Dice shown.** Every shot, yours and the enemy's, is logged and floated with the roll and the number needed (`MISS (5 vs 7+)`; a 1 reads `1 = miss` and a 10 `10 = hit`). Rocket heat shows as `+N HEAT`.

**Measured after.** The single-assault vs three-lights test drifted to 62%, 66% and 49% (it was 46, 56, 50 before the weapon retune), so heavies now have an edge in easy and normal; that is expected from the weapon identities and needs a play-test and a squad re-tune before it is called a problem.

**Open.** Does a loss now read as explainable? Does the AI actually reach water and woods on this map (a pond and woods near each side may be needed)? Not yet built: incoming-fire lines, an after-action summary, range rings, a threat preview on the move range finder. Mediums as a flexible pick need the mech bay to show up in play.

**Keyboard play.** Arrow keys and WASD move a hex cursor one hex a press, starting from the highlighted mech: up/down go straight, left/right zig-zag so repeated presses run level. In the move phase ENTER moves to the cursor, or stays put if the cursor is on the mech itself (SPACE and right-click also stay put); in the fire phase the arrow keys and WASD step through the valid targets (you fire at mechs, not scenery; TAB does the same), the mouse resting on a valid enemy aims at it, ENTER locks and SPACE skips. Mouse and keyboard share the one cursor. Test hook token `go` = SPACE in the move phase, ENTER in the fire phase.

**Sticky heat.** Before, heat barely mattered: in 60 AI-vs-AI fights no mech ever reached heat 8, because each heat sink removed a whole point a round. Now each sink removes `COOL_FACTOR` (0.5, rounded down) of a point, so heat carries over from round to round. A round's cooling is: sinks x 0.5, +2 for ending the move phase standing still (`STAND_COOLING`), +2 for firing nothing that round (`HOLD_FIRE_COOLING`), + water (5). So not moving and not firing both have a real benefit, and water is the big cooler (it was already, and it now matters more). In the AI fights, about 11% of mech-rounds now start at heat 8 or more.

**Balance consequence (not yet tuned).** One assault mech against three lights, same budget, now: single wins 2% (budget 140), 10% (120), 49% (100); before 62/66/49. The balance squads buy no extra heat sinks, so a lone heavy overheats; the mech bay's sinks (and the chassis sink counts) are the intended answer, and the balance builder should buy sinks before this number means anything. Left as is until play-tested.

## Smoke rockets (FOGGER)

The owner's idea, to counter lasers camping in water and as the classic *defensive* tool of a medium mech. A FOGGER (weapon 8, `kind: 'smoke'`, 4 shots, range 4/7/10, heat 1, 6 credits) is indirect and **always lands: no roll**. It lays a cloud on the target mech's hex and its neighbours (7 hexes) for `SMOKE_ROUNDS` = 2: a cloud fired in a round blocks the rest of that round (so the other side's fire phase) and the whole of the next, then clears at the end of that round.

- **Effect:** smoke blocks line of sight *through, into and out of* its hexes (`map.smoke`, a hex-key to rounds-left object, so it is copied with the state; `hasLineOfSight` checks it). Lasers and cannons cannot fire across it (`no-line-of-sight`); rockets can, with the usual +1 indirect penalty. So a mech inside a cloud is safe from direct fire and blind to direct fire itself, but can still fire rockets: the medium with rockets and smoke.
- **Aim:** any living mech in range, **friend, foe or the shooter itself** (a screen), through the normal target flow. The suggestion is the nearest enemy; with no enemy in range there is no suggestion (ENTER says so; SPACE skips). The callout reads `LANDS 100%`.
- **Damage:** a smoke weapon does no damage, so a mech with only smoke counts as having no damaging weapon (for the AI's self-destruct rule).
- **AI:** `aiSmokeTarget`: a mech with smoke screens itself when an enemy with a laser or cannon has a clear shot at it (once the cloud stands that shooter has no view, so it does not waste more), otherwise it blinds an enemy standing in water; else it holds the rocket.
- **Screen:** hazy hexes over the map (thinner on the last round), puffs and a `SMOKE` float where it lands, a log line.
- **Squad code:** weapon ids need 4 bits now (8 weapons), so the code went to version 2 (since superseded by version 3, see Weapon slots; no old codes existed).
- **Test hooks:** `?code=<squad code>` plays any squad (a medium with smoke: `2133-209E-0008-8200-0120-440F-5`); `?smoke=5,4;9,6` starts with clouds.

Open: how smoke should interact with water cooling; whether one cloud per mech per round is needed; a smoke-using enemy squad (none of the fixed enemy squads carry one yet); the 4-shot magazine; whether the AI should use smoke offensively on a mech about to fire rockets (it cannot stop rockets).

## Self-destruct

The owner's idea: a mech may blow itself up instead of firing, which also gives a mech with no weapons left something to do.

- **Rule:** in the fire phase a mech can self-destruct instead of firing (any mech, not only a weaponless one). Every living mech in the six hexes around it, **friend or foe**, takes `SELF_DESTRUCT_DAMAGE` (15) as an ordinary hit (a location is rolled in the advanced model; the pool takes it straight off), in id order, and then the bomber is destroyed (in the advanced model it loses its centre torso). It resolves with the volley: simultaneous with everyone else's fire, so a bomber killed by an earlier shot in the same volley still goes off, and the enemy cannot dodge it (they move first, but it goes off after the movement phases).
- **Plan:** a fire plan entry `{ mechId, destruct: true }`; the rules refuse one for a shut-down or destroyed mech, a duplicate, or a mech that also fires (`destruct-and-fire`).
- **UI:** `K` while a mech is being asked about. It drops that mech's locks and its other weapon questions (Back undoes it), and the mech shows its blast hexes in pulsing red with `BOOM`. A mech with no weapon that can fire gets one question of its own, `NO WEAPONS` (K = self-destruct, SPACE = carry on); a mech that has weapons but nothing in range gets no question, so it cannot self-destruct that round. Smoke counts as a weapon here, so a mech with only smoke is asked about its smoke.
- **AI:** a mech with no damaging weapon left self-destructs when more enemies than friends are beside it, and otherwise walks toward the nearest enemy (its fallback "close the distance" move) until it can.
- **Screen:** a big explosion and `SELF-DESTRUCT` on the bomber, then a blast on each neighbour (`-15`), and log lines.
- **Replaced the stalemate draw.** A weaponless mech now always has something to do, so the "nobody can fire, draw" rule is gone; a fight of weaponless mechs ends when one blows up. Both sides destroyed is still a draw.
- **Test hooks:** `?at=P1:6,4;E1:9,4` places mechs; `?freezeSide=player` with `?freezeFire`; `?title` also prints every mech's position.

Open: 15 damage is fixed, whatever the bomber's size (a heavy bomb is no bigger than a light one); a mech that has weapons but no target cannot detonate; whether the AI should also blow up a nearly dead armed mech.

## Weapon slots, capacity and the heavy weapons

**Rule (the owner's):** a mech makes **at most three attacks** a round, but heavier mechs should *feel* more brutal, through bigger attacks, not more of them ("lots of smaller lasers or a big howitzer; think EVE Online in a forest").

- **No fixed mounting positions.** A build lists exactly **three weapon slots** (`MAX_WEAPONS`, 0 = empty), and a mech's shots come from its arms and centre torso by slot number (`MOUNT_LOCS`: right arm, left arm, centre torso; the advanced hit-location model hangs each weapon on that limb).
- **Capacity.** Each chassis has a `capacity` (light 3, medium 5, heavy 6, assault 7) and each weapon a `size` (1 to 3); the build's weapons may not add up to more than the chassis can carry (`checkCapacity`, run by `validateSquad`; `makeMech` does not enforce it, so tests can build over-armed test mechs). Both Skitter and Jackal carry 3; they differ in speed, armour, sinks and cost.
- **Squad code** is version 3: three 4-bit weapon ids per mech, so a code is at most 22 digits.

- **LASER ARRAY:** one attack that is four beams: one to-hit roll, then the same cluster roll as rockets decides how many beams land, each for 3. It needs line of sight (smoke stops it). One dose of heat (5), so you cannot fire it every round. It spreads damage evenly, so it is steady, not swingy.
- **HOWITZER:** indirect (no line of sight, +1 without it, minimum range 4), a single 18-point shell, and **splash 6 on every mech in the six hexes around the target, friend or foe** (the self-destruct blast code). A very slow shell: its mover penalty is tripled (walk +3, run +6), so it punishes mechs that stand still (water campers, mechs in the woods). **A miss does nothing at all.** Smoke does not stop it. Its reach (11) is the longest in the game and the most the pace rule allows (no weapon may reach more than 60% of the map's width). The AI holds it when the splash would hit more of its own mechs than enemies.
- **Heavy only:** both need a chassis of capacity 6 or more (`HEAVY_MIN_CAPACITY`), error kind `too-heavy`.
- On screen: the aiming callout reads `splash 6 on every mech beside the target (friends too)` and shades the splash hexes orange; beams land one after another; the shell arcs in and hits with a big boom and shake.
- Not yet in any default or enemy squad (the mech bay is not built); try one with `?code=3135-22A4-0481-0012-0400-10` (a Warden with a howitzer and an array, and two Skitters).

**Open (the owner's idea, parked):** limit *targets* to three (one per arm, one for the torso) instead of attacks, with several weapons in a group firing at that group's target. It would give heavies many more attacks per round without more questions; it was left because the three-attack cap with bigger guns may already be enough. Revisit after play-testing.

**The COMBAT LOG (it was the TACTICAL READOUT) shows the last combat round.** After each combat round, both sides' actions and what they did (shots, hits, criticals, kills, the heat events at the end of the round) stay on screen, in order, until the next combat round begins: it clears when the next round's first volley starts, so you can read it through the movement phases and while you choose your own targets. The heading names the round (`ROUND 8: COMBAT`) and each side's fire gets its own coloured line (`YOUR FIRE`, `ENEMY FIRE`). It holds the latest 11 lines (oldest at the top), and before any combat it reads `NO COMBAT YET`. The per-weapon `no target` lines are no longer logged (the weapon list and prompt already say).

**One card and the combat log.** The right-hand panel shows a single mech card above a tall COMBAT LOG (about 25 lines, so a whole combat round fits). There is always one mech *in focus*: the mech that is acting, yours when you are being asked about it and the enemy's while it moves or fires in its turn (with the bright edge, and for yours the heat ghost and the weapon being aimed); and, temporarily, whichever mech the cursor is over, friend or foe, which takes the card until the cursor moves off. With neither, a hint (`HOVER A MECH FOR ITS STATS`). The three squad cards and the enemy card that used to replace the log are gone, so the log is never covered.
